You've sent your plans to three builders. You get back quotes of $380,000, $425,000, and $465,000. Which is the best deal?

The honest answer: you can't tell from those numbers alone. Builder bids are only comparable when they include the same scope.

## Why Bids Are Hard to Compare

### The Scope Problem

Builder A's $380,000 quote might exclude:

- Septic system
- Driveway
- Final grading
- Appliances
- Light fixtures (allowance only)
- Deck

Builder C's $465,000 quote might include all of these plus higher-quality finishes.

Without a detailed scope comparison, the lowest bid might actually be the most expensive.

### The Allowance Problem

Allowances are placeholders for costs that aren't fully specified. Common allowances include:

- Flooring
- Lighting fixtures
- Plumbing fixtures
- Appliances
- Countertops
- Cabinetry upgrades

A bid with $5,000 allowance for lighting looks cheaper than one with $12,000. But if your selections actually cost $15,000, the first bid is $10,000 short.

### The Specification Problem

"Vinyl plank flooring" can mean:

- $2/sq ft builder-grade product
- $5/sq ft mid-range product
- $8/sq ft premium product

Without specifications, you're comparing categories, not products.

## Creating Comparable Bids

### Define Your Scope Clearly

Before requesting bids, document:

**What's Included**
- All work to be performed
- All materials to be supplied
- All fixtures and finishes
- Site work and grading
- Utility connections
- Cleanup and waste removal

**What's Excluded**
- Items you'll supply
- Work by others
- Future phases

### Specify Materials and Finishes

For every category, provide:

- Product name or specifications
- Acceptable alternatives
- Quality tier required

**Example specification**:
> Flooring, main level: Luxury vinyl plank, 6mm+ thickness, waterproof core, 20mil+ wear layer. Product: [Brand/model] or approved equal.

### Set Realistic Allowances

If you must use allowances:

- Research actual costs first
- Set allowances at realistic levels
- Specify what the allowance covers
- Define how overages are handled

**Example allowance**:
> Lighting fixtures allowance: $8,500. Covers all interior and exterior lighting fixtures. Does not include installation labour. Unused allowance credited; overages invoiced at cost plus 10%.

## Evaluating Bids

### Create a Comparison Matrix

Build a spreadsheet with every scope item as a row:

| Item | Builder A | Builder B | Builder C |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Foundation | Included | Included | Included |
| Framing | Included | Included | Included |
| Roofing | Included | Included | Included |
| Septic | Excluded | $18,000 | Included |
| Driveway | Excluded | Excluded | Included |
| Appliances | $3,000 allow | Excluded | $6,500 allow |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |
| **Total as-bid** | $380,000 | $425,000 | $465,000 |
| **Adjustments** | +$35,000 | +$12,000 | +$0 |
| **Comparable total** | $415,000 | $437,000 | $465,000 |

### Normalize for Exclusions

For each excluded item:

1. Get a separate quote for that work
2. Add to the bid total
3. Or request the builder add it to their scope

### Normalize for Allowances

For each allowance:

1. Price your actual selections
2. Calculate the difference from allowance
3. Add overages to bid total

### Evaluate Specifications

For items with different specs:

1. Determine if the difference matters to you
2. Price the upgrade/downgrade
3. Adjust totals accordingly

## Red Flags in Bids

### Unusually Low Bids

A bid significantly below others often means:

- Misunderstanding of scope
- Missing items
- Unrealistic allowances
- Inexperience with similar projects
- Financial distress (dangerous)

### Vague Scope Descriptions

Watch for:

- "Allowance for finishes"
- "As per plans" (when plans don't specify)
- "Standard grade materials"
- "To be determined"

### Missing Line Items

Professional bids should itemize:

- Site preparation
- Foundations
- Framing and structure
- Roofing
- Exterior envelope
- Windows and doors
- Interior rough-in (electrical, plumbing, HVAC)
- Insulation and air sealing
- Interior finishes
- Exterior finishes
- Cleanup and waste removal

### No Change Order Process

Every bid should explain:

- How changes are priced
- Markup on changes
- Approval process
- Documentation requirements

## Questions to Ask Every Bidder

1. What's not included in this bid?
2. What allowances are you using and what do they cover?
3. When were materials last priced? Any price protection?
4. What's your change order process and markup?
5. What's your warranty coverage?
6. Who will be on-site daily?
7. What's your timeline?
8. What permits are included?
9. Who schedules inspections?
10. What documentation do I receive at completion?

## Making the Decision

After normalizing bids:

1. Eliminate any bidders you don't trust
2. Compare normalized totals
3. Consider qualitative factors:
   - Communication quality
   - References
   - Relevant experience
   - Schedule reliability
   - Problem-solving approach

The best bid isn't always the lowest. It's the one that gives you confidence in the final cost and quality.
