Your building site determines much of what follows. These twelve questions will help you understand what you're working with and what it means for your build.

## Access and Logistics

### 1. How will you access the site during construction?

**Why it matters**: Construction requires heavy truck access for concrete, lumber, and equipment delivery. Poor access increases costs and limits options.

**What to check**:
- Road width and weight capacity
- Seasonal limitations (spring breakup, winter conditions)
- Distance from paved road
- Any easements or right-of-way restrictions
- Turning radius for large vehicles

**Red flags**:
- Access only via seasonal road
- Narrow or winding approach
- Water crossings without rated bridges
- Access across others' property without legal easement

### 2. Where will materials be staged and stored?

**Why it matters**: You need space for material deliveries, often weeks before installation. Poor staging leads to damaged materials and inefficient construction.

**What to check**:
- Flat area for lumber storage
- Secure area for tools and equipment
- Distance from building location
- Drainage to prevent water damage

### 3. Is there cell service or internet access?

**Why it matters**: Communication during construction is essential. Remote sites without service create coordination challenges.

**What to check**:
- Cell coverage (walk the site with your phone)
- Internet options (satellite, cellular, rural ISP)
- Alternatives if coverage is poor

## Utilities and Services

### 4. How far is the electrical connection?

**Why it matters**: Utility connection costs scale dramatically with distance. A 200m power run can cost tens of thousands of dollars.

**What to check**:
- Distance to nearest transformer
- Single or three-phase power available
- Line extension costs from utility
- Temporary construction power options
- Off-grid alternatives if connection is impractical

**Reality check**: Get a written quote from the utility before committing to the property.

### 5. What's your water source?

**Why it matters**: Water options vary widely in cost, quality, and reliability. This affects both construction and long-term living.

**Options to evaluate**:
- Municipal water connection
- Well (shallow or deep)
- Surface water (requires treatment)
- Water delivery and storage
- Rainwater collection (supplemental)

**What to check**:
- Water quality test results from neighbours
- Typical well depths in the area
- Drilling costs from local companies
- Treatment requirements

### 6. What are your septic options?

**Why it matters**: Septic system type depends on soil conditions and lot size. Some sites require expensive engineered systems.

**What to check**:
- Perc test results (or have one done)
- Required setbacks from property lines and water
- Soil type and water table depth
- Conventional vs. engineered system likely
- Local health authority requirements

**Critical note**: Get a perc test before purchasing land. A failed perc test or requirement for a $50,000 engineered system changes everything.

## Site Conditions

### 7. What's the slope and drainage pattern?

**Why it matters**: Slope affects foundation costs significantly. A sloped site might need an expensive foundation or major site work.

**What to check**:
- Overall slope percentage
- Direction of natural drainage
- Flat area large enough for building plus setbacks
- Cut and fill requirements

**Cost impact**:
- Flat site: Standard foundation, lowest cost
- Gentle slope: Minor adjustments, modest increase
- Moderate slope: Stepped or walkout foundation, significant increase
- Steep slope: Major engineering, substantial cost increase

### 8. What's the soil composition?

**Why it matters**: Soil type affects foundation design, septic feasibility, and excavation costs. Problem soils can add tens of thousands to your budget.

**What to check**:
- Surface soil observation
- Geotechnical report (if required)
- Neighbours' experiences
- Rock near surface
- High water table

**Problem soils**:
- Expansive clay
- Organic/peat soils
- High water table
- Shallow bedrock
- Fill material

### 9. What vegetation will you clear or keep?

**Why it matters**: Trees provide wind protection, privacy, and aesthetic value. But too many trees too close create fire risk and maintenance burden.

**What to consider**:
- Building envelope clearing requirements
- FireSmart zone requirements
- Trees to preserve for privacy/wind protection
- Clearing costs
- Stump and debris removal

## Environment and Risk

### 10. What natural hazards affect this site?

**Why it matters**: Northern BC sites may face multiple hazards that affect design, insurance, and safety.

**Hazards to assess**:
- Wildfire interface (very common in BC)
- Flood plain location
- Slope stability
- Snow loads (varies by location)
- Wind exposure
- Seismic zone

**How to check**:
- Local government hazard maps
- Insurance requirements
- Regional district development permits
- Neighbours' experiences

### 11. What wildlife considerations exist?

**Why it matters**: Wildlife affects landscaping choices, food storage, waste management, and daily life.

**Common considerations in northern BC**:
- Bear activity (food storage, waste management)
- Ungulates (fencing for gardens)
- Beavers (drainage management)
- Nesting birds (timing of clearing)

### 12. What are the solar and wind conditions?

**Why it matters**: Solar exposure affects passive heating, PV potential, and snow melt. Wind exposure affects heat loss and comfort.

**What to observe**:
- South-facing exposure (critical for passive solar)
- Shading from terrain or vegetation
- Prevailing wind direction
- Wind protection from terrain or trees

**Design implications**:
- Window placement and sizing
- Building orientation
- Need for wind breaks
- Roof snow management

## Using Your Answers

### Summary Assessment

After answering these questions, you should understand:

1. **Feasibility**: Is this site buildable at all?
2. **Cost factors**: What site-specific costs will you face?
3. **Design implications**: How does the site affect your plan choice?
4. **Risk profile**: What ongoing challenges will you manage?

### Red Flags Checklist

Reconsider the site if:

- [ ] No legal access or unclear easements
- [ ] Utility connection costs exceed 10% of building budget
- [ ] Failed or marginal perc test
- [ ] Significant geotechnical concerns
- [ ] Multiple unmitigated hazards
- [ ] Site work costs exceed 15% of budget

### Green Lights

Good signs include:

- [ ] Legal year-round access
- [ ] Utilities available at property line
- [ ] Good perc test results
- [ ] Gentle slope with good drainage
- [ ] Stable soils confirmed
- [ ] Manageable hazard profile

## Next Steps

Once you've assessed your site:

1. Get written quotes for utility connections
2. Commission perc test if not done
3. Consider geotechnical report if soils are questionable
4. Review findings with your plan selection
5. Adjust budget for site-specific costs

Your site assessment shapes every decision that follows.
