Building your own home in northern BC is one of the most rewarding projects you can undertake. It's also one of the most complex. This guide walks you through the complete journey from first idea to move-in day.

## Phase 1: Discovery and Planning

Before you spend money on land or plans, invest time in understanding what you're getting into.

### Key Questions to Answer

- **Budget reality check**: What can you actually afford? Factor in land, construction, soft costs, and a healthy contingency.
- **Timeline expectations**: Most self-builds take 12-24 months from breaking ground. Plan accordingly.
- **Your involvement level**: Will you be an owner-builder, work with a GC, or something in between?
- **Site requirements**: What kind of land suits your plan? Access, utilities, and terrain all matter.

### What to Do in This Phase

1. Establish a realistic budget range (we recommend the Budget Sanity Checklist)
2. Research land options in your target area
3. Talk to people who have built in your region
4. Review plan options to understand what's feasible

## Phase 2: Site Selection and Acquisition

Your land choice determines much of what follows. Don't rush this decision.

### Critical Site Factors

- **Access**: Year-round road access matters for construction and living
- **Utilities**: Distance to power, water availability, septic feasibility
- **Terrain**: Slope affects foundation costs significantly
- **Zoning**: Verify what you can build before purchasing
- **Natural hazards**: Flood plains, fire interface, unstable slopes

### Due Diligence Checklist

- [ ] Zoning verification with local government
- [ ] Utility availability confirmation
- [ ] Septic feasibility assessment
- [ ] Geotechnical considerations
- [ ] Survey and legal boundaries
- [ ] Access rights and easements

## Phase 3: Design and Planning

With your site secured, it's time to finalize what you're building.

### Plan Selection

Starting with a proven plan dramatically reduces risk and cost compared to custom design. Look for:

- Appropriate size for your needs
- Foundation options that suit your site
- Design suited to northern climate
- Layout that works for your lifestyle

### Customization Decisions

Most plans allow modification. Prioritize changes that:

- Address site-specific requirements
- Accommodate must-have features
- Stay within structural constraints
- Don't balloon the budget

### Permitting Requirements

BC building permits require:

- Stamped architectural drawings
- Stamped structural engineering
- Site plan showing setbacks
- Septic design (if applicable)
- Energy compliance documentation

## Phase 4: Procurement and Contracting

How you buy and who you hire determines much of your build experience.

### Procurement Approaches

**General Contractor**: One contract, one point of responsibility. Higher cost, lower management burden.

**Owner-Builder**: You hire trades directly. More work, more control, potential cost savings.

**Hybrid**: GC for structure, owner manages finishes. Balance of control and convenience.

### Getting Comparable Bids

The biggest procurement mistake is comparing incomparable quotes. Ensure:

- Same scope in each bid
- Clear inclusions and exclusions
- Defined allowances with realistic amounts
- Change order process specified

## Phase 5: Construction

With plans, permits, and contracts in place, construction begins.

### Pre-Construction Checklist

- [ ] Building permit posted on site
- [ ] Utility connections arranged
- [ ] Site access prepared
- [ ] Temporary facilities (power, sanitation)
- [ ] Construction schedule established
- [ ] Communication protocols agreed

### During Construction

- Document everything with dated photos
- Track all changes in writing
- Schedule inspections as required
- Maintain organized records
- Address issues promptly

### Common Challenges

- Weather delays (especially in northern BC)
- Material availability and lead times
- Subcontractor scheduling conflicts
- Scope changes and their cost impacts
- Inspection failures and corrections

## Phase 6: Completion and Move-In

The final phase requires attention to detail.

### Pre-Occupancy Checklist

- [ ] Final inspection passed
- [ ] Occupancy permit issued
- [ ] All warranties documented
- [ ] As-built drawings received
- [ ] Utility accounts established
- [ ] Insurance in place

### Warranty Period

Your new home warranty covers:

- Materials and labour: 2 years
- Building envelope: 5 years
- Structure: 10 years

Document issues promptly and in writing.
